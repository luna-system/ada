Positioning Ada in Left-Leaning Spaces

An Exploration in XF Praxis

Status: Draft exploration, not yet public
Date: December 2025
Context: How do we talk about Ada in communities that (rightfully) distrust AI?

The Tension

The communities that would most benefit from Ada - folks who care about privacy, autonomy, resisting surveillance capitalism, owning their tools - are (rightfully!) the most skeptical of AI in general.

Why the Left Distrusts AI (And Should)

Valid critiques that Ada doesn’t negate:

  • Training data theft from artists/writers

  • Worker displacement without safety nets

  • Concentration of power in Big Tech

  • Extractive business models (pay-per-token rent-seeking)

  • Surveillance capitalism on steroids

  • Environmental costs of massive compute

  • “AI” as excuse for unaccountable decisions

  • Hype culture obscuring real harm

Why Ada Exists

Ada exists BECAUSE those critiques are correct.

Every design decision is a response to that landscape:

Critique

Ada’s Response

Enclosure of the commons

CC0 license - truly public domain

Surveillance capitalism

Local-first - no cloud dependencies

Extractive business models

No API costs - run on your hardware

Black-box unaccountability

Hackable - readable code, documented patterns

Compute waste

Runs on old hardware, CPU-only mode exists

Data mining

No telemetry - your data never leaves

Intentional obscurity

High-quality docs for humans AND machines

The XF Frame: “Neither Clean Hands Nor Pure Hearts”

From the Xenofeminist manifesto (see docs/xenofeminism.rst):

“If nature is unjust, change nature!”

The refusal of purity politics applies here:

The Impurity

We can’t deny these realities:

  • LLMs trained on scraped data (can’t undo that)

  • Massive compute already spent (energy already burned)

  • Built on infrastructure made by corporations (Docker, GitHub, Python)

  • Even the hardware requires extractive mineral supply chains

The Intervention Anyway

  • Take these artifacts of extraction

  • Build commons-based tools with them

  • Refuse the landlord relationship

  • Make it hackable so others can fork and modify

  • CC0 it so there’s no IP enclosure

  • Document it so others can learn and build

“Neither clean hands nor pure hearts” means:

You don’t get to wait for ethical AI training before building tools for autonomy. The models exist NOW. People are paying rent to OpenAI NOW. The enclosure is happening NOW.

So you build with what you have, toward the world you want.

Messaging That Might Land

1. “They’re Enclosing Features That Should Be Free”

Memory, search, tools - these aren’t innovations, they’re basic utilities. Charging $20/month for semantic search over your own conversations is absurd. Ada is about breaking the enclosure.

2. “You Should Own Your Means of Production”

If AI is a tool (it is), workers should own the tools. Not “democratizing AI” (VC speak), actually putting it in your hands. No landlord between you and your assistant.

3. “Privacy Is Resistance”

Your conversations are strategy, organizing, personal life. Every API call is potential surveillance. Local-first isn’t just convenience, it’s security culture.

4. “Not Open-Source-With-A-Roadmap-To-SaaS”

CC0 means no pivot to paid tiers. No “community edition vs enterprise.” Actually in the commons, forever. No rug-pull possible.

5. “Reuse, Don’t Consume”

Works on 5-year-old hardware. CPU-only mode exists. Degrowth-compatible tech. Not “AI requires latest $2000 GPU.”

The Real Pitch

“We agree with your critique of AI. That’s why we built this. Not as an alternative product, but as an argument that these features shouldn’t be products at all. They should be utilities, like grep or ssh, that you run on hardware you control. Ada is what AI looks like when it’s not designed for extraction.”

Where This Gets Tricky

We can’t avoid:

  • “But AI training data is still stolen” (it is)

  • “But you’re still using LLMs trained on massive compute” (true)

  • “You’re not ethically pure” (no one is)

But we can say:

“Yeah, and? The models exist. They’re trained. That ship sailed. The question now is: who gets to use them, and on what terms? Should everyone pay rent to OpenAI forever, or should we build commons-based tools? We choose the latter.”

The Honest Take

You’re not going to convince everyone.

Some folks’ AI critique is “this shouldn’t exist at all.” That’s valid! We’re not arguing with that position.

But for folks whose critique is “this shouldn’t be controlled by billionaires” - those are your people.

What Ada Actually Is

The solarpunk AI stack:

  • Runs on old hardware

  • Respects privacy

  • Stays in the commons

  • Puts users in control

  • Extensible by design

  • No planned obsolescence

The Rally Cry

Not marketing. Not a pitch. A statement of intent:

“Let’s build tools that let weird kids make weird things.” 💜

Tactical Positioning

Ada isn’t “ethical AI” (no such thing, not really).

Ada is tactical:

  • Harm reduction

  • The tools exist - who gets to use them?

  • Purity politics means only rich people get tools

  • Given we’re all already complicit, what do we build?

That’s XF praxis.

Next Steps

  • [ ] Don’t claim purity - claim better outcomes

  • [ ] Center autonomy, privacy, and commons over “innovation”

Notes

The backlash will come. That’s okay. The goal isn’t universal acceptance. The goal is to reach people who:

  • Understand the critique

  • Need the tools anyway

  • Want to resist extraction

  • Believe in building alternatives

Those people exist. They’re building mutual aid networks, running community servers, organizing digitally. They need secure, private, autonomous tools.

That’s who Ada is for.

Personal note: Just because we’re scared of backlash doesn’t make things less urgent. The enclosure is happening. We build anyway.
